# Four MI processes: observation guide (curated summary)

Source: Rubrik Penilaian SafeSesi v1, section 2.5 (an adaptation based on Miller & Rollnick,
2023, chapters 2 and 4–7). This is not a MITI score and produces no numbers.

General rules (Rubrik 2.5):
- The processes overlap and can recur; one exchange may show more than one process.
- Read counselor turns together with the client's responses. Do not decide from a single word,
  a question mark, or the position in the session.
- Behavior codes are not mapped one-to-one to processes.
- An observation describes visible activity; it does not prove the relationship worked, that the
  client is ready, or that the student mastered the process.
- If evidence is insufficient, report the process with `evidence_found: false`.

## Engaging (Miller & Rollnick ch. 4)
Building and keeping a respectful working relationship.
- Look for: inviting the client's perspective and experience; showing understanding through
  listening or reflection; responding to client discomfort to repair the relationship.
- Limits: an opening greeting alone is not enough; reflections can serve other processes too.

## Focusing (ch. 5)
Developing and keeping a direction about change.
- Look for: exploring what the client wants to discuss or change; weighing priorities when there
  are several concerns; clarifying or checking the direction with the client.
- Limits: naming a topic or switching questions is not enough. An agreed focus is recorded only
  with evidence from both sides; the counselor's own choice is not an agreement.

## Evoking (ch. 6)
Drawing out the client's own motivation for change.
- Look for: eliciting desire, reasons, need and ability to change; following up the client's
  change language; linking change to the client's values or hopes.
- Limits: exploring the problem in general is not necessarily evoking; counselor-supplied reasons
  differ from eliciting the client's; spontaneous change talk does not by itself prove an evoking
  effort.

## Planning (ch. 7)
Developing commitment and a way to carry out change, with the client involved.
- Look for: inviting steps the client wants to try; discussing time, method, support or obstacles;
  checking fit and readiness.
- Limits: one-sided advice is not collaborative planning; talking about steps does not prove
  readiness. Sessions are not required to reach planning.
